# Brand comparison and buying guidance

The generated tables in [`generated/brand-comparison.md`](generated/brand-comparison.md)
contain every number. This document explains how to read them and what the pattern
means.

**This is not an endorsement of any brand.** Products were selected to span price
tiers, forms, and certification status. Twenty products are a sample, not a
ranking of the market.

## 2. How cost is calculated

Cost is normalised to **2000 IU D3 and 100 mcg MK-7 per day**. A 5000 IU product
is dosed at 0.4 capsules per day; a 180 mcg MK-7 product at 0.56 capsules per day.
This is the only way to compare a 30-count bottle with a 360-count bottle, and it
is why the headline price is often misleading.

Standalone MK-4 is compared at its label serving because retail doses (45 mcg to
15 mg) are not commensurate with trial doses (45 mg/day).

### The three numbers that matter

1. **Cost per 30 days at the reference regimen** — what a month actually costs.
2. **Cost per 1000 IU D3** or **per 100 mcg MK-7** — the unit price, which exposes
   pack-size and dose tricks.
3. **Certification** — the only cheap proxy for label accuracy.

## 3. Vitamin D3: value tiers

| Tier | Product | $/30 days | $/year | Certification |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Budget | NOW 2000 IU, 240 softgels, $13.59 | $1.70 | $20.67 | None documented |
| Budget | NOW 2000 IU, 120 softgels, $7.19 | $1.80 | $21.87 | None documented |
| Mid | Carlson 2000 IU, 360 softgels, $29.90 | $2.49 | $30.32 | None documented |
| Mid | Carlson 2000 IU, 120 softgels, $12.90 | $3.23 | $39.24 | None documented |
| Premium | Thorne D-5000, 60 capsules, $20.00 | $4.00 | $48.67 | NSF Certified for Sport |

**What you get for the premium:** certification, a smaller pill count, and brand
consistency. Thorne's D-5000 is NSF Certified for Sport, which includes
banned-substance screening and label verification. If you are a tested athlete or
want the strongest available quality signal, that is worth $28 a year. If you are
not, the budget product delivers the same molecule at a fifth of the cost.

**What you do not get:** better absorption, better efficacy, or a different form.
All five are cholecalciferol.

### The dose trap

Thorne D-5000 is a 5000 IU capsule. At the reference regimen of 2000 IU/day it
lasts 150 days, which is why its monthly cost looks reasonable. But most people
taking it will take one capsule a day, which is 5000 IU/day — above the tolerable
upper limit for long-term use. That is acceptable as a short correction course,
not as indefinite maintenance without monitoring.

The same applies to Nature Made's 5000 IU and 10,000 IU products and to most
"extra strength" SKUs. **The dose on the label determines whether the product is
appropriate, not the price per bottle.**

The spread is **11×** between the cheapest and most expensive way to buy 100 mcg of
MK-7 per day. That spread is not explained by dose, because all of these deliver
100–180 mcg. It is explained by pack size, brand, and certification.

The Nature Made product is the only USP Verified plain MK-7 in the sample, and it
is also the most expensive per dose because it comes in a 30-count bottle. That is
a rational trade only if certification matters more to you than an order of
magnitude in cost — and even then, buying a larger certified pack or a certified
combination would be cheaper.

### The MK-4 exception

Thorne's MK-4 liquid costs $75 for 80 servings of 15 mg, or about $0.94 per
serving. That is cheap per milligram compared with MK-7, but the doses are not
comparable: MK-7 is active at micrograms because of its long half-life, while MK-4
requires milligrams. At the Japanese trial dose of 45 mg/day, this product would
cost about $2.80/day. It is a niche product for people specifically following an
MK-4 protocol.

## 5. Combinations: paying more for less evidence

| Product | D3 | K2 | $/30 days | $/year |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Thorne D + K2 liquid, 600 servings | 1000 IU | 200 mcg MK-4 | $3.40 | $41.37 |
| NOW D3 & K2, 120 capsules | 1000 IU | 45 mcg MK-4 | $5.62 | $68.38 |
| Sports Research D3 + K2, 60 softgels | 5000 IU | 100 mcg MK-7 | $12.98 | $157.98 |
| Nature Made D3 + K2, 30 softgels | 5000 IU | 100 mcg MK-7 | $16.79 | $204.28 |
| Nature Made D3 + K2 gummies, 50 count | 5000 IU | 100 mcg MK-7 | $25.19 | $306.45 |

Median combination cost is **4.5×** the median D3-only cost. For that premium you
get a nutrient whose added benefit over D3 alone has not been demonstrated for any
hard outcome, and whose best-powered trial found no bone benefit [R24].

The Thorne liquid is the value outlier because 600 servings per bottle is
exceptional. But two drops supply only 1000 IU of D3, so reaching 2000 IU means
four drops a day, and the MK-4 dose (200 mcg) is far below any trial dose.

### The gummy problem

Gummies are the most expensive format per dose in this sample and the format most
prone to potency problems. Two of 26 vitamin D products in ConsumerLab's 2024
testing contained nearly twice their labelled dose [R46], and chewables are harder
to manufacture consistently than softgels. If you take a gummy, take it because
you will actually take it — not because it is a good value.

## 6. Certification: what it is worth

| Mark | Products in sample | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USP Verified | Nature Made K2 | Identity, potency, purity, dissolution, GMP |
| NSF Certified for Sport | Thorne D-5000 | NSF/ANSI 173 plus banned-substance screening |
| NSF/ANSI 173 | Thorne D + K2 liquid | Label claims, contaminants, toxicology, GMP |
| None documented | 17 products | Brand claims only |

Independent testing has found real failures: 52–135% of expected potency across 55
bottles in one analysis [R48], two products at nearly twice their label dose in
another [R46], and significant deviations across 25 combined D3/K2 products in a
third [R49]. Certification does not eliminate risk, but it is the only cheap,
verifiable signal available to a consumer.

**"Third-party tested" without a named laboratory or public listing is
marketing.** Several products in this sample make that claim. None names a
laboratory that publishes results.

## 7. What to buy

### If you want vitamin D only

- **Best value:** a 2000 IU softgel or capsule in the largest pack you will
  finish, from a brand with certification if the price premium is small.
  In this snapshot, NOW 2000 IU 240-count at $13.59 is $0.03 per 1000 IU.
- **Best certified:** Thorne D-5000 at $20 for 60, if certification matters. Take
  one capsule every other day to approximate 2500 IU/day, or use it as a
  correction dose.
- **Avoid:** 10,000 IU products for maintenance; gummies for precision;
  "liposomal" or "micronised" premiums without 25(OH)D data.

### If you want K2

- **Best value:** Horbaach 100 mcg 250-count at $15.49, if you accept no
  certification.
- **Best certified:** Nature Made K2 100 mcg, USP Verified, accepting the small
  pack and higher cost.
- **Best middle ground:** Sports Research 180 mcg at $18.95, which delivers one
  capsule per day at the dose used in the positive BMD trial.
- **Avoid:** MK-4 products marketed as equivalent to MK-7; "K complex" products
  if you take warfarin; any K2 product making cardiovascular claims.

### If you want both

Buy them separately. A 2000 IU D3 capsule plus a 100 mcg MK-7 capsule costs about
$3–8 per month at the values above, versus $13–25 for a combination — and you
control the doses independently.

## 8. Price collection caveats

- Prices are US one-time retail snapshots collected 2026-11-18, excluding tax and
  shipping.
- Several brand-store prices were promotional and are labelled as sale prices.
- Two products were priced through an independent tracker because the brand page
  did not expose a price. Those rows are labelled `Third-party price check`.
- Certification status reflects public listings as of the collection date; brands
  add and lose certifications.
- Pack sizes change frequently. Re-check the source URL before buying.
