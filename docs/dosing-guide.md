# Dosing guide

The short version: **600–800 IU/day of vitamin D3 meets requirements for most
adults; 800–2000 IU/day is a reasonable supplemental range; 4000 IU/day is the
upper limit.** Take it daily, with a meal. K2 is optional and is not dosed for a
proven outcome.

All recommendations assume **cholecalciferol (D3)** and healthy adults. See the
[safety guide](safety-guide.md) for who should not self-supplement.

---

## 1. Maintenance doses

| Group | RDA | Practical supplemental dose | Upper limit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Infants 0–12 months | 400 IU | 400 IU | 1000–1500 IU |
| Children 1–8 years | 600 IU | 600–1000 IU | 2500–3000 IU |
| Children 9–18 years | 600 IU | 600–1000 IU | 4000 IU |
| Adults 19–70 years | 600 IU | 600–2000 IU | 4000 IU |
| Adults 71+ | 800 IU | 800–2000 IU | 4000 IU |
| Adults 75+ (2024 guideline) | — | 400–3333 IU, average ~900 IU | 4000 IU |
| Pregnancy/lactation | 600 IU | 600–2000 IU | 4000 IU |

The 2024 Endocrine Society deliberately did not specify an optimal dose, because
"the optimal doses for empiric vitamin D supplementation remain unclear" [R02].
The ranges above are the trial ranges it reviewed, not a prescription.

## 2. Choosing a dose

| Your situation | Starting dose | Recheck |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Healthy, regular sun exposure, good diet | 600–1000 IU/day | Not required |
| Limited sun, indoor work, high latitude, winter | 1000–2000 IU/day | Optional |
| Dark skin at high latitude | 1000–2000 IU/day | Optional |
| Age 75+ | 800–1000 IU/day | Annually |
| Obesity (BMI >30) | 2000–4000 IU/day | 3 months |
| Malabsorption or bariatric surgery | 3000–6000 IU/day or specialist protocol | 3 months |
| Documented deficiency (<20 ng/mL) | Correction course | 8–12 weeks |
| Osteoporosis on treatment | 800–2000 IU/day | Per clinician |
| Chronic kidney disease | Nephrology protocol | Per nephrologist |

Obesity deserves emphasis: adipose tissue sequesters vitamin D, and BMI above 25
attenuates the 25(OH)D response to a given dose [R43]. Doubling the usual dose is
a reasonable starting point, with a recheck rather than an assumption.

## 3. Correcting deficiency

Common clinical regimens, all requiring follow-up:

| Regimen | Dose | Duration | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly high dose | 50,000 IU once weekly | 8 weeks | Then 1500–2000 IU/day |
| Daily high dose | 6000 IU/day | 8 weeks | Then 1500–2000 IU/day |
| Moderate daily | 2000–4000 IU/day | 12 weeks | For mild insufficiency |
| Monthly high dose | 50,000–100,000 IU monthly | 2–3 months | Higher variability; not preferred |

Recheck 25(OH)D after 8–12 weeks, then adjust maintenance. Do not continue a
correction dose indefinitely; 50,000 IU weekly is roughly 7000 IU/day, which
exceeds the upper limit as a maintenance intake.

**Avoid single annual boluses.** A 500,000 IU annual dose increased falls
(IRR 1.15) and fractures (IRR 1.26) in older women, with the excess in the first
three months [R17]. Daily dosing is both safer and more effective for falls
prevention [R61].

## 4. Vitamin K2 dosing, if you choose to take it

| Form | Dose used in trials | Retail dose | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MK-7** | 90–720 mcg/day | 45–180 mcg | 180 mcg used in the positive BMD trial [R23]; 375 mcg in the null trial [R24] |
| **MK-4** | 45 mg/day (Japan) | 45 mcg–15 mg | Retail doses are far below the trial dose; not equivalent to MK-7 |
| **K1** | 500–1000 mcg/day | 90–120 mcg (dietary) | Not the form marketed for bone or arteries |

There is no RDA for K2. The adequate intake for total vitamin K is 90 mcg/day for
women and 120 mcg/day for men, based on K1 [R39]. Most adults eating vegetables
and dairy already meet it.

If you take K2:

- Choose MK-7 90–180 mcg/day, with a meal containing fat.
- Prefer a product that declares all-trans content or uses a branded ingredient
  such as MenaQ7.
- **Do not take it if you use warfarin or another vitamin K antagonist.**
- Do not expect a measurable clinical benefit; the evidence is not there.

## 5. Timing

**Any consistent time of day works.** What matters is consistency and, modestly,
taking it with fat.

- Fat increases peak absorption of a single D3 dose by about 32% [R41], although
  one trial found no difference in 25(OH)D at 90 days [R42].
- A meal containing at least 15 g of fat is a reasonable target.
- Evening dosing has no proven advantage for vitamin D; the vitamin D–sleep
  literature is weak.
- K2 is also fat-soluble; take it with the same meal.

The practical rule: attach both to a meal you already eat every day.

---

## 6. Missed doses

- Take the missed dose when you remember, unless it is close to the next one.
- Do **not** double up. Vitamin D is stored in fat and liver; a double dose adds
  nothing useful and slightly increases hypercalcemia risk.
- A missed week is not a crisis. 25(OH)D has a half-life of two to three weeks,
  so levels fall slowly.
- After a correction course, missed maintenance doses matter more than missed
  doses during loading.

## 7. What to expect

| Timeframe | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 weeks | 25(OH)D begins to rise |
| 8–12 weeks | New steady state on a given dose |
| 3–6 months | Bone turnover markers normalise if deficient |
| 1–3 years | Small BMD changes, if any |
| Never | An acute energy, mood, or immunity boost in a replete person |

Vitamin D is not a stimulant and does not produce a perceptible effect in someone
who is already sufficient. If you feel dramatically better on it, consider whether
you were deficient — or whether something else changed.

## 8. Dosing mistakes

1. **Taking 5000–10,000 IU as maintenance.** That is a correction dose. Long-term
   use raises hypercalcemia risk without proven benefit [R52; R54].
2. **Using annual or monthly boluses for convenience.** Associated with more falls
   and fractures, and no better correction [R17].
3. **Not taking it with food.** Costs you roughly a third of peak absorption.
4. **Stacking a multivitamin, a D3 capsule, and a D3+K2 combo.** Easy to reach
   6000–8000 IU/day unintentionally.
5. **Taking K2 with warfarin.** INR can fall substantially [R37].
6. **Assuming a higher 25(OH)D is better.** Above 30–40 ng/mL there is no
   demonstrated benefit, and above 50 ng/mL there is signal for harm in some
   observational analyses [R55].
7. **Buying gummies for precision.** Potency varies more in chewable formats
   [R46].
8. **Expecting K2 to replace osteoporosis treatment.** It has never been shown to
   prevent fractures.

## 10. A practical default

If you are a healthy adult who wants a simple, defensible regimen:

1. **Vitamin D3 1000–2000 IU once daily with breakfast.** One bottle of 240
   softgels lasts four to eight months and costs about $14–20.
2. **No routine blood test** unless you have a risk factor or symptoms.
3. **No K2** unless you have a specific reason and are not anticoagulated.
4. **Recheck** only if you have a condition that affects absorption or
   metabolism, or if you develop symptoms of hypercalcemia.
